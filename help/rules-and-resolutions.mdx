Prediction markets depend on clear rules and reliable resolution. This page explains how Futuur markets are created and resolved, what sources are used to determine outcomes, why some markets may be canceled, and when users receive their payouts.

## How are markets created?

Markets on Futuur are proposed around real-world future events. Futuur has hundreds of active markets covering virtually any topic imaginable.

Each market is structured around a question with a verifiable outcome and resolution criteria defined before the market opens, so everyone knows exactly what is being predicted and how the result will be determined.

## How does Futuur resolve a market?

When the event takes place, Futuur checks the outcome using the resolution source defined in the market rules and confirms which outcome occurred.

Positions on the correct outcome become worth the full payout, while positions on incorrect outcomes lose their value.

## What counts as a resolution source?

The resolution source is the official and public reference used to confirm the outcome of an event. For example, this may be an electoral authority, a sports league, a government agency, or a recognized news outlet.

Each market defines its resolution source in the rules before it opens, and it is always recommended that you read these criteria before trading, since they determine how ambiguous situations will be handled.

## Why are some markets canceled?

A market may be canceled when its outcome cannot be determined fairly — for example, if the event does not happen, is postponed indefinitely, or if the question becomes ambiguous or impossible to resolve using the defined source.

When this happens, positions are unwound and the amounts wagered are returned to participants.

## When do I receive my payout?

Your payout is credited to your balance as soon as the market is resolved and the official result is confirmed by the resolution source.

In general, this happens shortly after the result becomes known, but it may take longer if there is a delay in the publication of official data or if the market rules specify a determination date later than the apparent end of the event.
