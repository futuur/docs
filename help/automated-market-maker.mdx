One of Futuur’s most distinguishing pieces is our use of an Automated Market Maker (AAM) to determine prices. Please read on to learn more about what it is and how it works in our markets.

## What is an Automated Market Market (AMM)?

An Automated Market Maker (AMM) is an algorithm that allows us to provide a buy or sell price for any outcome at any time, even when a market's [Order Book](/help/order-book) is empty.

The use of an Order Book/AMM hybrid approach has the advantage of instant, always-on liquidity for all of our markets, while at the same time maintaining the benefits of dynamic, exchange-based pricing. That ensures trades are always possible, even in markets where the order book alone fails to provide enough liquidity.

## How does the AMM work?

Futuur uses the [LS-LMSR](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~sandholm/liquidity-sensitive%20automated%20market%20maker.teac.pdf) (Liquidity-Sensitive Logarithmic Scoring Rule) market maker, an approach that builds on the foundation of the original LMSR algorithm, widely used in prediction market applications. Characteristics of the LS-LMSR include the ability to set a bounded loss and to set a commission on trades. In addition, because it is “liquidity-sensitive”, as liquidity increases there will be less price movement (slippage) with each incremental trade. That is, higher volume markets will show less price slippage.

All markets are initiated with an amount of shares distributed amongst each of the outcomes, which provides the initial liquidity. The price of an outcome is determined by a cost function calculated based on the distribution of shares. To calculate the purchase price, the previous cost function is subtracted from the new one.

To calculate the sale price, Futuur uses the Always Moving Forward approach, which means that rather than decreasing the shares in the sold outcome, the shares in the other outcomes are increased by an equivalent value. This is applied when there is market activity between the original purchase and the sale event; it is not applied to a sale by the same user that occurs immediately after a purchase.
